# **Side Projects**

Since middle school, I’ve always had some form of side project. Over time, my interests have evolved from robotics in undergrad to photography, then amateur radio, and now,slowly getting back into robotics (thanks to my robot vacuum).

With the advent of low-cost electronics and near-free shipping from Taobao and AliExpress, I’ve accumulated quite a number of DIY components for various projects. And with AI-assisted coding tools becoming mainstream, I have fewer excuses to procrastinate. But despite all the tools, the biggest challenge remains: **finishing** the side projects.

# **Why Do Personal Projects Stall?**

From my experience (and I'm sure many can relate), here are some common reasons:

1. **Shiny New Project Syndrome** – It’s easy to get distracted by a new idea.  
2. **Time constraints** – Life gets in the way.  
3. **Scope creep** – One small idea quickly balloons into a complex system.  
4. **Loss of motivation** – Without a deadline or audience, priorities shift.

Hardware-based side projects are especially difficult. Software can usually be paused and resumed relatively easily. But hardware needs space, setup time, and physical debugging. These barriers can discourage short, spontaneous work sessions.

At work, we’re guided by structure: project plans, deadlines, and meetings. That pressure focuses effort toward delivery. But side projects are often unstructured and reward-driven. Discipline becomes the driving force—and that’s harder to sustain consistently.

Small issues can lead to deep rabbit holes. Often, I lose sight of the original goal. This post is also a reminder to myself: **Stay focused on the outcome, even while exploring the interesting detours.**


# **This Blog’s Purpose**

Over the years, I’ve learned a lot from blogs, tutorials, forums, and YouTube channels. Even if you follow someone else’s guide step by step, you’ll often run into unexpected issues. Sharing those deviations can help the next person or feed an AI model somewhere that might one day suggest it as a solution. This blog is a way of giving back to the DIY community.

Apart from that, it’s also an attempt to build a lightweight system of **accountability and documentation**, and to provide closure—even for partially finished projects. It can also help build momentum through **visible progress**.
